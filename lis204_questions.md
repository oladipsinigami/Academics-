### QUESTION 01
Type: Essay
Category: Compulsory Class Assignment
Text: (a) MIS in Academic Library Decision-Making: Analyze how a Management Information System (MIS) assists library managers and university archivists in making evidence-based administrative decisions regarding budget allocation, collection usage, and staffing.
(b) Case Study — FUTA Library (Albert Ilemobade Library): Critically evaluate the practical operational impact of implementing a modern MIS in an academic technology library such as the Federal University of Technology, Akure (FUTA) Library. Highlight improvements in circulation metrics, serials management, and electronic resource tracking.
(c) MIS vs. Library Automation System: Differentiate clearly between a Library Automation System (e.g., Koha, Voyager ILS) and a Management Information System (MIS) in an academic library setting. Explain why automating library routines does not automatically constitute an MIS unless analytical decision-support tools are embedded.
(d) Strategic Planning Process for MIS: Outline the sequential phases of the Strategic Information System Planning (SISP) process in designing an MIS for a university library, detailing the factors (corporate changes, competitive opportunities/threats, and system evolution) that initiate SISP.
Model Answer: (a) A Management Information System (MIS) transforms raw library transactional data (circulation counts, OPAC search logs, gateway turns, e-journal downloads) into actionable managerial intelligence. It assists library managers and archivists by optimizing resource allocation, collection development, and staff deployment.
(b) At FUTA Library (Albert Ilemobade Library), an integrated MIS enables automated tracking of e-resource databases (IEEE Xplore, ScienceDirect), predictive circulation management for high-demand textbooks, and download analytics for faculty publications in the institutional repository.
(c) A Library Automation System (ILS) handles routine operational transactions (checkout, cataloging). An MIS synthesizes operational data into executive summary reports and decision support for leadership.
(d) SISP is a top-down planning process initiated by corporate changes, external opportunities/threats, and system evolution. Its phases include: 1. Analyzing organizational objectives; 2. Assessing current IT infrastructure; 3. Formulating MIS architecture; 4. Implementation roadmap and evaluation.

### QUESTION 02
Type: Essay
Category: Methodology
Text: (a) Classroom Interaction Questions: In class, the lecturer raised two fundamental questions regarding Information System Development (ISD) methods:
i. Why are local methods developed?
ii. Are textbook methods actually used in practice?
Evaluate the contradiction between textbook ISD methods and real-world practice in library automation, explaining why academic libraries frequently modify standard methods or develop proprietary local methods.
(b) Tacit Method Knowledge: Define Tacit Method Knowledge in the context of library information system development. Explain how reflection-in-action, intuition, and technical rationality influence system development.
(c) Degrees of Method Modification: Explain the three (3) evaluated degrees of method modification in library MIS:
1. Selection path within a method (Modification Exemplifier - ME)
2. Combination of methods for internal use
3. Organization of locally developed proprietary methods
Model Answer: (a) Textbook ISD methods assume rigid, linear development paths, whereas real-world library automation requires flexible, localized adaptations to unique workflows, institutional constraints, and legacy data structures. Libraries develop local methods to fill gaps left by generic textbook frameworks.
(b) Tacit Method Knowledge is the experiential, intuitive understanding developed by systems librarians and developers through practice, reflection-in-action, and practical problem-solving rather than formal textbook manuals.
(c) 1. Selection path: Selecting specific subsets/phases of an established method. 2. Combination: Blending features of multiple methods to create a hybrid internal framework. 3. Proprietary local methods: Engineering custom, localized development procedures built specifically for the institution.

### QUESTION 03
Type: Essay
Category: Management Structure
Text: (a) Four Functional Types of MIS: Define and compare the four (4) functional types/levels of MIS described in the lecture notes:
1. Data Bank Management Information System (DMIS)
2. Predictive Management Information System
3. Decision-Taking Management Information System
4. Decision-Making Management Information System
(b) Library Management Hierarchy & Roles: Outline the 3-tier Library Management Hierarchy (Top Management, Middle Management, Operational/Lower Level), detailing specific staff titles, responsibilities, and functions across each tier.
(c) Communication Mechanics: Analyze the downward, upward, and horizontal communication flows required between these management levels to ensure strategic library goals are achieved without bottlenecking.
Model Answer: (a) DMIS: Centralized database storage of basic records. Predictive MIS: Uses historical data and statistical models to project future trends. Decision-Taking MIS: Programmed system that automatically executes decisions based on predefined rules (e.g. locking patron accounts when fine limits are reached). Decision-Making MIS: Evaluates multi-criteria options and presents strategic recommendations for human executive selection.
(b) Top Management (University Librarian, Deputy Librarians): Strategic planning, policy formulation, budget defense. Middle Management (Heads of Units - Reference, Serials, Cataloging): Operationalizing policies, unit supervision, resource requests. Operational Level (Library Officers, Assistants, Porters): Daily routine execution (charging, shelving, patron assistance).
(c) Downward communication conveys administrative goals and directives; Upward communication delivers operational reports, feedback, and resource requests; Horizontal communication ensures inter-departmental synergy between technical, circulation, and IT units.

### QUESTION 04
Type: Essay
Category: IT Ethos
Text: Explain the three (3) core pillars of the Ethos of Information Technology in library operations:
1. Complexity: The internal architecture and system designer specifications of library IT.
2. Speed: The storage, retrieval, and transmission capabilities for massive digital collections.
3. Predictability: Why information technology is inherently less predictable and reliable when compared to traditional pervasive utilities (electricity, automobiles, television), and how library managers mitigate system downtime.
Model Answer: 1. Complexity: Refers to the multi-layered technical infrastructure, network protocols, database schemas, and MARC/Dublin Core metadata standards powering library systems.
2. Speed: The rapid processing capability for indexing millions of bibliographic records, instant search queries, and high-speed digital document transmission.
3. Predictability: IT systems are prone to software bugs, server crashes, cyber threats, and network outages, making them less predictable than physical utilities. Managers mitigate this via automated backups, cloud redundancy, disaster recovery plans, and maintenance SLA protocols.
