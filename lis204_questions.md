## MODEL ANSWERS & TECHNICAL MARKING SCHEME

### MODEL ANSWER FOR QUESTION 1 (COMPULSORY EXAM QUESTION)

#### (a) MIS in Academic Library Decision-Making:
A Management Information System (MIS) transforms raw library transactional data (circulation counts, OPAC search logs, gateway turns, e-journal downloads) into actionable managerial intelligence. It assists library managers and archivists by:
1. **Resource Allocation & Budgeting:** Analyzing cost-per-use of subscribed databases to decide renewals versus cancellations.
2. **Collection Development:** Tracking high-demand subject areas and patron reservation queues to guide book procurement.
3. **Staffing Optimization:** Identifying peak library foot-traffic and virtual query hours to schedule reference desk librarians efficiently.

#### (b) Case Study — FUTA Library (Albert Ilemobade Library):
In an institution like FUTA, implementing an integrated MIS yields:
1. **Automated E-Resource Tracking:** Monitoring usage of IEEE Xplore, ScienceDirect, and AGORA to ensure ROI on subscriptions.
2. **Predictive Circulation Management:** Detecting high-demand engineering textbooks and automatically flagging them for short-term reserve loans.
3. **Institutional Repository Analytics:** Measuring worldwide citations and downloads of FUTA faculty research publications.

#### (c) MIS vs. Library Automation System:
- **Library Automation System (ILS):** Focuses on routine operational transactions (e.g., checking out a book at the circulation desk, cataloging a new acquisition using MARC21). It processes day-to-day data.
- **Management Information System (MIS):** Aggregates, synthesizes, and analyzes operational data from the ILS to produce summary reports, trend analyses, and decision support for executive library leadership (University Librarian). Automation executes tasks; MIS evaluates and guides decisions.

#### (d) Strategic Information System Planning (SISP) Process:
SISP is a disciplined, top-down approach ensuring IT infrastructure aligns with university strategic goals.
- **Initiating Factors:** (1) Major institutional changes (e.g., launching new academic faculties); (2) External opportunities/threats (e.g., cyber threats, open-access mandates); (3) System evolution (legacy hardware obsolescence).
- **Phases:** (1) Analyzing organizational objectives; (2) Assessing current IT infrastructure; (3) Formulating MIS architecture (hardware, software, data, personnel); (4) Implementation roadmap & evaluation.

### MODEL ANSWER FOR QUESTION 3 (LEVELS OF MANAGEMENT & MIS TYPES)

#### (a) Four Functional Types of MIS:
1. **Data Bank MIS (DMIS):** Centralized storage of organizational records where data fields may duplicate across files for rapid access (e.g., student registration records linked with library patron files).
2. **Predictive MIS:** Uses historical trend data and statistical modeling to generate forecasts relevant to policy making (e.g., predicting next year's inter-library loan demand).
3. **Decision-Taking MIS:** An automated system programmed with predefined rules to make routine decisions directly without human intervention (e.g., automatic system lock on patron accounts upon reaching fine limits).
4. **Decision-Making MIS:** Incorporates organizational value systems and complex criteria to present evaluated options and recommendations for human executive selection.

#### (b) Library Management Hierarchy Table:
| Management Level | Library Staff Designations | Core Management Functions & Budgetary Roles |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Top Management** | University Librarian, Deputy University Librarians | Long-term strategic planning, institutional budget defense, policy formulation, capital investments, and senior appointments. |
| **Middle Management** | Heads of Units (Reference, Cataloging, Acquisition, Serials, Systems) | Tactical execution of top-level policies, workflow supervision, unit budget management, staff appraisal, and resource ordering. |
| **Operational / Lower** | Library Officers, Library Assistants, Clerical Staff, Porters, Secretaries | Day-to-day routine execution (shelving, book charging/discharging, security clearance, patron registration, typing). |
