# LIS 210 Question Bank - Knowledge Organization & Library Classification (Based Strictly on Lis 210.pdf Notes)


---

### QUESTION 01
Type: Essay
Category: Easy
Text: Define Library Classification according to the LIS 210 course notes.
Model Answer: According to the LIS 210 lecture notes, Library Classification is defined as the systematic arrangement of human knowledge and recorded information—in books and non-book documents deposited in the library (such as serials, tapes, microfilms, maps, and computer disks)—into specific subject classes (e.g. Religion, Economics, Education, Agriculture) placed in different parts of the library in a logical sequence based on subject analysis.

---

### QUESTION 02
Type: Essay
Category: Easy
Text: State the primary aims and objectives of library classification as outlined in the lecture notes.
Model Answer: The primary aims and objectives of library classification are: (1) To ensure the maximum use of library materials; (2) To decrease time lost by library users and staff when searching for books; (3) To arrange books in a helpful, logical order on the shelf; and (4) To achieve subject collocation by grouping books on the same or related subjects together upon request.

### QUESTION 03
Type: Essay
Category: Easy
Text: List S.R. Ranganathan's Five Laws of Library Science as highlighted in the LIS 210 notes.
Model Answer: Ranganathan's Five Laws of Library Science are: (1) Books are for use; (2) Every reader his or her book; (3) Every book its reader; (4) Save the time of the reader; and (5) The library is a growing organism.

---

### QUESTION 04
Type: Essay
Category: Easy
Text: List and define the five facets of S.R. Ranganathan's PMEST formula used in faceted classification.
Model Answer: Ranganathan's PMEST formula stands for: (1) P - Personality: The most fundamental and main focal facet in a subject classification; (2) M - Matter: The physical or material substance of the subject; (3) E - Energy: The action, process, operation, or problem; (4) S - Space: The geographical location or territory; and (5) T - Time: The chronological period or era.

### QUESTION 05
Type: Essay
Category: Easy
Text: Highlight four key benefits of library classification to library users and staff as stated in the notes.
Model Answer: Four key benefits are: (1) It helps library users and staff easily locate books and information on the shelves; (2) It helps staff return consulted or returned books back to their exact relative places; (3) It assists librarians in identifying gaps in the library collection; and (4) It saves a great deal of time when conducting reference searches.

### QUESTION 09
Type: Essay
Category: Easy
Text: State the rule of classification regarding 'Subject versus Form' when classifying a book.
Model Answer: The fundamental rule of classification states: 'Class a book by its subject first, and then by its form.' Subject content takes precedence over the physical or literary presentation form.

### QUESTION 10
Type: Essay
Category: Easy
Text: Explain the classification rule regarding 'Specific Subject' assignment.
Model Answer: The rule states: 'Class a book under the most specific subject heading/class rather than under a broad general class.' An item should be placed in the narrowest class that encompasses its complete scope.

### QUESTION 11
Type: Essay
Category: Hard
Text: Analyze the rule of utility in classification: 'Class a book where it will be most useful.' Explain how this principle guides a classifier when a book touches multiple subjects.
Model Answer: The overarching principle of library classification is to class a book where it will be most useful to library readers. When a book addresses multiple subjects or interdisciplinary topics, the classifier must assess the primary target audience, local library environment, and user reading background. If a book covers two or three subjects, it is classed under the first or most heavily emphasized subject; if it covers more than three subjects, the rule dictates placing it in a broader general class where patrons searching for that general field will find it most useful.

### QUESTION 12
Type: Essay
Category: Hard
Text: Detail the complete step-by-step procedure a classifier must follow when classifying a new book, according to the LIS 210 notes.
Model Answer: The LIS 210 notes specify a 5-step classification procedure: Step 1: Determine exactly the true subject of the book by examining the title, table of contents, preface, blurb, or text; Step 2: Consult the Classification Index to identify the appropriate main class or heading; Step 3: Consult the Classification Schedules to find the exact class number corresponding to the subject; Step 4: If greater specificity is required, consult Auxiliary Tables to synthesize numbers; Step 5: Write the finalized classification class number clearly on the flyleaf/title page of the book.

### QUESTION 13
Type: Essay
Category: Hard
Text: Critically discuss how Ranganathan's 4th Law ('Save the Time of the Reader') and 1st Law ('Books Are for Use') directly justify the necessity of systematic library classification.
Model Answer: Ranganathan's 1st Law ('Books Are for Use') establishes that unorganized books stored randomly hinder accessibility. Classification transforms passive storage into an active resource by logically organizing materials by subject on open shelves. Ranganathan's 4th Law ('Save the Time of the Reader') mandates that library routines should minimize patron search effort. Classification fulfills this by assigning unique location call numbers and collocating related books together so readers find their required materials without delay.

### QUESTION 14
Type: Essay
Category: Hard
Text: Evaluate the significance of the Publisher's Blurb and Introduction/Preface in clarifying an author's intention and point of view during subject analysis.
Model Answer: When title and table of contents are insufficient, the Introduction/Preface provides crucial context regarding the author's stated intention, scope, methodology, and target audience. The Publisher's Blurb (on the dust jacket or cover) provides a concise summary of the core subject matter prepared by subject editors. Together, they allow the classifier to confirm difficult or interdisciplinary subjects before assigning a class number.

### QUESTION 15
Type: Essay
Category: Hard
Text: Explain how library classification helps librarians manage collection development and identify 'Subject Gaps' in the library.
Model Answer: By grouping books systematically into specific subject classes on shelves and catalog registers, classification visually and statistically reveals the concentration of library holdings. Librarians can easily observe which subject areas are well-stocked and which subjects have few or outdated titles ('subject gaps'), enabling targeted selection and acquisition of new materials.

### QUESTION 16
Type: Essay
Category: Hard
Text: Examine the 'Multi-Subject Rule' in classification: How should a classifier handle a book that covers more than three distinct subjects?
Model Answer: According to the LIS 210 lecture notes, when a book deals with more than three distinct subjects, placing it under any single specific subject would hide its broader coverage from users interested in the other topics. Therefore, the classifier must apply the multi-subject rule and place the book in a broader general class that encompasses all the individual subtopics.

---

### QUESTION 17
Type: Essay
Category: Hard
Text: Discuss how Ranganathan's PMEST Facet Analysis (Personality, Matter, Energy, Space, Time) enables precise subject synthesis compared to traditional enumerative lists.
Model Answer: Traditional enumerative classification schemes attempt to list all pre-combined subjects in advance, which leads to rigidity when new or complex topics emerge. Ranganathan's PMEST facet analysis breaks a complex subject down into five fundamental facets: Personality (the primary concept/entity), Matter (material composition), Energy (operation/action), Space (geographic location), and Time (period). By combining these separate facets using specific connecting symbols, a classifier can synthesize precise class marks for newly emerging topics.

---

### QUESTION 18
Type: Essay
Category: Hard
Text: Explain how proper shelf classification reduces book loss and misplaced volumes in a library environment.
Model Answer: Systematic classification assigns every book a permanent relative position on the shelf. When readers or staff remove books, the classification call number ensures that returned books can be accurately replaced in their exact relative positions. This prevents misplaced books (which are effectively 'lost' to readers) and maintains helpful shelf order.

---

### QUESTION 19
Type: Essay
Category: Hard
Text: Contrast the primary function of the Classification 'Index' versus the Classification 'Schedules' during the number building process.
Model Answer: The Classification Index serves as an alphabetical relative guide listing subject terms alongside candidate class numbers; its primary function is to direct the classifier to the correct region of the system. The Classification Schedules represent the main hierarchical tables where class numbers are displayed in systematic logical order with scope notes and instructions. A classifier uses the Index first for guidance, but must always verify and finalize the class number in the Schedules.

### QUESTION 20
Type: Essay
Category: Hard
Text: Synthesize the relationship between Ranganathan's 5th Law ('The Library is a Growing Organism') and the hospitality requirement of a classification system.
Model Answer: Ranganathan's 5th Law recognizes that libraries continually expand in physical volumes, digital resources, and expanding fields of knowledge. To accommodate this growth without collapsing or requiring total re-classification, a classification system must possess high 'hospitality'—the structural capacity to insert new subjects and sub-topics at any point in the schedule without disrupting existing class arrangements.
