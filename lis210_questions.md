### QUESTION 11
Type: Essay
Category: Hard (2024/2025 Exam Aligned)
Text: (a) Analyze the Rule of Utility in library classification: 'Class a book where it will be most useful.' Explain how this principle guides a classifier when a book touches multiple subjects or interdisciplinary domains. (10 marks)
(b) Explain the 'Multi-Subject Rule': How should a classifier handle a book that covers two or three distinct subjects versus a book covering more than three subjects? (10 marks)
Model Answer: (a) The overarching principle of library classification is the Rule of Utility: placing a book where it will provide maximum retrieval value to readers. When an item touches multiple interdisciplinary subjects, the classifier assesses the target audience, primary subject emphasis, and institutional curriculum focus. The book is classed under the dominant subject while access points for secondary subjects are provided in the Web-OPAC.

(b) Handling Multi-Subject Works:
- Two or Three Subjects: Class the book under the first subject mentioned, or under the subject treated most comprehensively by the author.
- More Than Three Subjects: Classing the book under any single narrow topic hides its broader scope. Therefore, the Multi-Subject Rule mandates placing the item under a broader general class (e.g. a book covering Physics, Chemistry, Biology, and Geology is placed under 500 General Natural Sciences in DDC or Q General Science in LCC).

### QUESTION 12
Type: Essay
Category: Hard (2024/2025 Exam Aligned)
Text: (a) Detail the complete 5-step procedure a cataloguer/classifier must follow when analyzing and classifying a new book in a technical services unit. (10 marks)
(b) Contrast the primary function of the Classification 'Index' versus the Classification 'Schedules' during the notation assignment process. (10 marks)
Model Answer: (a) The 5-Step Classification Procedure:
1. Subject Analysis: Determine the precise subject content by examining title, table of contents, preface, and text.
2. Consult the Index: Search the classification index to identify candidate class headings and entry numbers.
3. Verify in Schedules: Locate the candidate numbers in the main Classification Schedules to verify hierarchy, scope notes, and classification rules.
4. Synthesize Notation: If required, consult auxiliary tables (e.g. area tables, standard subdivisions) to build a precise class number.
5. Write Call Number: Inscribe finalized call numbers clearly on the title page verso, flyleaf, and book spine label.

(b) Classification Index vs. Schedules:
- Classification Index: An alphabetical pointer listing subject terms alongside candidate class marks. It acts strictly as an initial navigational guide to direct the classifier to relevant schedule sections.
- Classification Schedules: The authoritative, hierarchically ordered tables containing main classes, divisions, subdivisions, and scope notes. A classifier must always verify candidate numbers in the Schedules before assigning a finalized call mark.

### QUESTION 15
Type: Essay
Category: Hard (2024/2025 Exam Aligned)
Text: (a) Define a Library Catalogue. Detail Cutter's three primary objectives of a library catalogue. (10 marks)
(b) Chronologically trace the physical forms of library catalogues from Book Catalogs and Card Catalogs to Microform (COM) and modern Web-OPAC portals. (10 marks)
Model Answer: (a) Library Catalogue & Cutter's Objectives: A Library Catalogue is a systematic register of all holdings in a library, providing access points to locate physical/digital items. Charles Ammi Cutter's 3 Objectives:
1. To enable a person to find a book of which either the Author, Title, or Subject is known.
2. To show what the library has by a given author, on a given subject, or in a given kind of literature.
3. To assist in the choice of a book as to its edition (bibliographic description) or character.

(b) Evolution of Physical Forms:
1. Book Catalogue: Handwritten or printed inventory bound in book volumes; static, difficult to update.
2. Card Catalogue: Standard 3x5-inch index cards filed in wooden drawers; flexible updating but single-user access.
3. Microform (COM) Catalogue: Reduced photographic film (microfiche/microfilm) read via projection devices.
4. Web-OPAC: Computerized relational database accessible 24/7 over web browsers, supporting multi-user concurrent keyword searching.

### QUESTION 16
Type: Essay
Category: Hard (2024/2025 Exam Aligned)
Text: (a) Differentiate between a Dictionary Catalogue and a Classified Catalogue in terms of structure, index reliance, user accessibility, and subject collocation. (10 marks)
(b) Evaluate the operational advantages and disadvantages of filing catalog cards in a single A-Z alphabetical sequence versus a two-part classified sequence. (10 marks)
Model Answer: (a) Dictionary vs. Classified Catalogue:
- Dictionary Catalogue: All entries (Author, Title, Subject, Cross-references) are interfiled into a single A-Z alphabetical sequence. Highly intuitive for lay users searching specific names or terms, but scatters related subjects alphabetically (e.g. Botany under B, Plants under P).
- Classified Catalogue: Divided into two parts: (1) Main Classified Part arranged numerically/alphabetically by classification call numbers; (2) Alphabetical Index Part (Author/Title/Subject index). Matches physical shelf order and preserves logical subject relationships, but requires consulting the index first.

(b) Comparative Advantages:
- Dictionary A-Z Sequence: Easy single-step lookup for users knowing exact author/title; however, filing rules for complex names/homonyms are difficult to maintain.
- Classified Sequence: Allows logical browsing of complete subject fields and matching shelf collocation; however, requires two-step searching (Index -> Call Number -> Entry).

### QUESTION 18
Type: Essay
Category: Hard (2024/2025 Exam Aligned)
Text: (a) Critically compare the Dewey Decimal Classification (DDC) system and the Library of Congress Classification (LCC) system across notation, structure, enumeration, and expansion capacity. (10 marks)
(b) Explain why major academic and technology university libraries (such as FUTA) adopt the Library of Congress Classification (LCC) system instead of the Dewey Decimal Classification (DDC). (10 marks)
Model Answer: (a) DDC vs. LCC System Comparison:
- Notation: DDC uses Pure Numeric Decimal Notation (000–999 with decimal extensions); LCC uses Mixed Alphanumeric Notation (A–Z letters for main classes combined with numbers).
- Main Class Structure: DDC is constrained by 10 main decimal classes; LCC utilizes 21 main classes (A to Z, excluding I, O, W, X, Y), providing vastly larger structural capacity.
- Flexibility & Number Length: Complex academic topics in DDC yield long, unwieldy decimal numbers (e.g. 025.43209669); LCC yields shorter, highly granular class marks (e.g. Z696.D7).

(b) Why FUTA Library Adopts LCC: FUTA (Albert Ilemobade Library) is a specialized academic technology university holding hundreds of thousands of complex, highly specialized scientific and technological treatises. LCC is specifically engineered for large academic research libraries: it offers 21 expansive main classes, shorter call numbers for complex interdisciplinary topics, continuous schedule updating by the Library of Congress, and superior shelf expansion capacity.

### QUESTION 19
Type: Essay
Category: Hard (2024/2025 Exam Aligned)
Text: (a) Define what a 'See Reference' is in the cataloguing process, detailing its cross-referencing mechanics and authority control function. (10 marks)
(b) Distinguish clearly between a 'See Reference' (0-entry pointer) and a 'See Also Reference' (related active heading pointer) using practical library cataloguing examples. (10 marks)
Model Answer: (a) 'See Reference' Mechanics: A **See Reference** (Directional Cross-Reference) is an authority control catalog entry that directs searchers from an unassigned, non-preferred variant term to the single official heading under which all bibliographic records for that subject or author are catalogued. Function: Standardizes entry points for pseudonyms, variant spellings, and synonyms (e.g., *Clemens, Samuel Langhorne SEE Twain, Mark*; or *Avian Influenza SEE Bird Flu*).

(b) See Reference vs. See Also Reference:
- See Reference: Connects a non-preferred term containing ZERO catalogue entries to the preferred active heading (*Non-preferred -> Preferred*).
- See Also Reference: Connects two related, active preferred headings that BOTH contain catalogued books (*Active Heading A SEE ALSO Active Heading B*, e.g., *Agriculture SEE ALSO Farming*; or *Information Technology SEE ALSO Computer Networks*).

### QUESTION 37
Type: Essay
Category: 2024/2025 Semester Examination (Section A)
Text: Highlight ten (10) practical guides and rules for effective cataloguing in a library technical services unit. (10 marks)
Model Answer: Ten practical rules for effective cataloguing:
1. Attentiveness & Concentration: Focus fully during cataloguing to eliminate typographical errors in bibliographic records.
2. Absolute Accuracy Over Speed: Prioritize exactness; a catalogue error may cause a book to remain lost on library shelves for years.
3. Strict Adherence to Standards: Follow established code rules (AACR2 / RDA / MARC21) consistently across all entries.
4. Primary Verification of Sources: Always examine the Title Page, Verso, Table of Contents, and Preface rather than relying solely on cover titles.
5. Use Pencil for Initial Worksheets: Write draft cataloguing cards/worksheets in pencil to facilitate easy proofreading and correction before final entry.
6. Maintain Authority Files: Consult name and subject authority files to ensure authorized forms of names and subject headings are used.
7. Consistent Subject Heading Choice: Assign standard Library of Congress Subject Headings (LCSH) or Sears List headings without creating informal ad-hoc terms.
8. Document Decisions: Maintain a Cataloguing Decision File to preserve local policy precedents when cataloguing staff change over time.
9. Verification of Call Numbers: Verify new classification numbers against the shelf list to prevent assigning duplicate call numbers.
10. Proofread Final Entries: Carefully review computer OPAC records or typed cards against the physical item before releasing books to circulation.

Text: Define what a 'See Reference' is in the cataloguing process, explaining its function, cross-referencing mechanics, and distinction from a 'See Also Reference'. (10 marks)
Model Answer: A **See Reference** (also known as a Directional Cross-Reference) is an authority control entry in a library catalogue that directs a user from a heading or term under which no bibliographic entries are filed to the preferred standard heading under which all items on that topic or author are catalogued.
- **Function**: Guides searchers from variant spellings, pseudonyms, discarded forms, or synonymous terms to the single authorized access point (e.g., *Twain, Mark SEE Clemens, Samuel Langhorne*; or *Avian Flu SEE Bird Flu*).
- **Distinction from 'See Also Reference'**: A 'See Reference' connects an unassigned term to an assigned heading (0 entries -> actual records). A 'See Also Reference' connects two related assigned headings that both contain active catalogue records (e.g., *Agriculture SEE ALSO Farming*).

### QUESTION 41
Type: Essay
Category: 2024/2025 Semester Examination (Section B)
Text: What are the key differences between a Dictionary Catalogue and a Classified Catalogue in terms of arrangement, user access points, indexing, and shelf collocation? (20 marks)
Model Answer: Comparative Analysis:
1. Arrangement:
   - **Dictionary Catalogue**: All entries (Author, Title, Subject, Cross-references) are interfiled together in a single continuous alphabetical sequence from A to Z, like a standard dictionary.
   - **Classified Catalogue**: Divided into two distinct parts: (a) A Classified Section arranged systematically by classification call numbers; and (b) An Alphabetical Index Section (Author, Title, Subject) guiding users to the call numbers.
2. User Friendliness & Searching:
   - **Dictionary Catalogue**: Highly intuitive for lay users looking up specific authors, titles, or direct subject terms without needing to know a classification system.
   - **Classified Catalogue**: Superior for advanced subject researchers because all related subtopics and logical class relationships are grouped together systematically in call number sequence.
3. Shelf Collocation Alignment:
   - **Dictionary Catalogue**: Separates related subjects alphabetically (e.g. 'Botany' under B, 'Plants' under P, 'Flora' under F).
   - **Classified Catalogue**: Matches the exact logical shelf arrangement of books on physical library shelves (subject collocation).

   - To enable a person to find a book of which either the Author, Title, or Subject is known.

   - To assist in the choice of a book as to its edition or character (descriptive cataloguing).
2. Historical Physical Forms:
   - Book Catalogue (Printed list bound in volumes);
   - Card Catalogue (Standard 3x5 inch catalog cards filed in wooden drawers);
   - COM Catalogue (Computer Output Microform requiring microfiche readers);
   - Web-OPAC (Modern online interactive searchable database accessible via web browsers).

### QUESTION 43
Type: Essay
Category: 2024/2025 Semester Examination (Section B)
Text: Critically discuss the differences between the Dewey Decimal Classification (DDC) system and the Library of Congress Classification (LCC) system, comparing notation, structure, flexibility, and suitability for academic libraries. (20 marks)
Model Answer: Comprehensive Comparison:
1. Notation:
   - **DDC**: Uses Pure Decimal Numeric Notation (000–999) consisting entirely of Arabic numerals with decimal extensions (e.g. 025.43).
   - **LCC**: Uses Mixed Alpha-Numeric Notation combining capital letters for main classes (A–Z) and numbers for subdivisions (e.g. Z695.Z8).
2. Main Class Structure & Expansion:
   - **DDC**: Structured around 10 rigid main classes based on decimal division. Highly suitable for public and school libraries, but can result in very long, unwieldy class numbers for complex academic subjects.
   - **LCC**: Utilizes 21 main classes (A to Z, omitting I, O, W, X, Y). Offers massive enumeration capacity and shorter call numbers, making it the preferred choice for large academic and research libraries like FUTA.
3. Revision & Maintenance:
   - **DDC**: Periodically revised in numbered editions (e.g. 23rd Edition) by OCLC; flexible for small to medium collections.
   - **LCC**: Continuously updated in separate published schedules for each main class by the Library of Congress, providing deep, specialized subject granularity.
